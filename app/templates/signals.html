How the ratio is computed

The proportional reporting ratio, the standard first-pass screen in pharmacovigilance. For a term in one study, over a 2×2 table:

this term other terms
this studyab
other studiescd

PRR = (a / (a+b)) ÷ (c / (c+d))

Screening threshold: PRR ≥ {{ threshold }} with at least {{ case_floor }} cases. The case minimum is not padding — with one case the ratio is arithmetically enormous and epidemiologically meaningless, which the Arthralgia row above demonstrates at a PRR of 14.

What this does not say

  • It is not an incidence rate. The denominator is other reports, not patients exposed. Disproportionality can only say a term is over-represented relative to the rest of this dataset.
  • It is not causation. A signal is a hypothesis for a human to investigate. Causality assessment on each individual case — the WHO-UMC category on every event — is a separate judgement made by a clinician.
  • The volumes here are small. Real practice adds a chi-square or a confidence interval on the PRR; at these counts both would be noise, so the case count is reported plainly instead of being dressed in statistics it cannot support.
  • Uncoded events are excluded from both sides of the ratio rather than counted as a category — an absence of information should not quietly depress every ratio in the portfolio.
